Tasks a single agent with tools could have handled more simply

  • Ticket 102 from start to finish. One agent with get_lease, check_cash_for_rent, and preview_payments could have checked the notice, checked the cash, and proposed the rent in about 3 tool calls. Our team used 11 tool calls, 13 model requests, and 29,620 tokens, and repeated the Accounting work.
  • The stock check in 101. check_order_fulfillment already returns the stock, the other sizes, the linked invoice, and the vendor lead time in one call. Sending that to a separate Inventory agent, then asking Inventory again from Accounting, added steps without adding facts.
  • The math in 103. Margin and discount numbers come straight from evaluate_bulk_discount. One agent could have run it at 10%, 15%, and 20% and shown me a small table. Splitting it across Accounting and Customer Service meant the 62% test was run twice and the useful range never appeared.
  • Where separate agents did help: keeping the customer reply separate from the money discussion (neither draft mentions cash, rent, or unit cost), and having Accounting look at all bills together. That is how rent stayed first and the $840 invoice still fit, ending at $160 instead of going negative. For a three-ticket shop, though, one agent with clear rules would have been cheaper: 95,025 tokens for three tickets is a lot for these answers.

Three more business problems our current agents could solve

These use tools and data we already have. The agents would still only propose, and I would approve.

  1. Answering stock questions for other products. check_stock works for any SKU we give it. For example, the crest mug (CC-MUG-CREST) is at 0 and the blue hat (CC-HAT-BLUE) has 40, and list_vendors shows Elm City Gifts restocks mugs and small goods in 3 days. Inventory and Customer Service could draft honest replies like they did for 101.
  2. A cash and bills check before spending anything. With get_cash_position, list_vendors, and preview_payments, Accounting can show what's owed, what's overdue, and what a payment would leave. That matters now because we only have $160 left in checking. Any new spending, like restocking hoodies for 103, would need to be checked first.
  3. Margin checks on any discount request. evaluate_bulk_discount works for any price-override ticket. With a prompt that asks for a few realistic discount levels, Accounting could give me a clear "safe range" for 103 and for future bulk orders.

Three problems they can't solve without new tools or capabilities

  1. Getting more stock. The agents can see that 103 is short 12 hoodies, but there's no tool or table to place a vendor order or reserve stock, and no hoodie restock lead time in our data. The ticket stays stuck until a person orders more.
  2. Talking to customers. Every customer message is a draft by design, and there's no send tool. The replies to Tauhid (101) and the Yale AI Club (103) are still waiting for a person, and the agents can't read the customer's answer when it comes back. Both open tickets depend on that answer.
  3. Recording what actually happens after a decision. The agents can't record a sale, take items out of inventory, track money coming in, or update the lease. For example, after we paid rent, leases.next_due still says 2026-09-02, because there's no tool to move it to the next month. Closing 101 and 103 properly would need order and inventory updates that don't exist yet.

What I'd keep and what I'd change

  • Keep: human approval on money. A wrong approval code was refused and paid nothing, both payments needed my code, and checking went $3,400 → $1,000 → $160 without ever going negative. The original database stayed unchanged the whole time.
  • Keep: honest statuses. Only 102 is Resolved, and 101 and 103 show what's blocking them instead of being forced closed.
  • Change: stop duplicate hand-offs (the double Accounting check on 102), make Customer Service offer alternative sizes and never put tool names in customer text, and have Accounting test realistic discounts.
  • Change: the dashboard clears the approval code on reload, which is why my first two status updates never reached the backend. It should tell me clearly when Apply is disabled.
